wirth's law — software bloat

Software grows faster than hardware improves

“Software is getting slower more rapidly than hardware becomes faster.”
— Niklaus Wirth, A Plea for Lean Software, 1995 (Wikipedia — Wirth's Law)
planned obsolescence

Not all obsolescence is natural

NATURAL

Technology improves, old hardware genuinely can't keep up. The gap between what software demands and what the device can do becomes real.

ENGINEERED

Companies deliberately limit device lifespan through software updates, parts unavailability, or policy — to drive new purchases.

the debate

The debate

The case for rapid advancement
  • You get more for your money than ever. A $400 laptop today does what a $3,000 machine from 2000 couldn't. (Morrow)
  • Devices now last longer on a charge — power efficiency improved dramatically alongside speed.
  • Computing was genuinely democratized. Things that required expensive specialists are now free and instant.
The case against rapid advancement
  • The era of automatic improvement is ending — but upgrade pressure isn't. “The golden age of more rapidly cheapening transistors did not survive unchallenged past the new millennium.” (Flamm, NBER)
  • Software intentionally absorbs every hardware gain. Your new device feels as slow as your old one within two years. (Wirth's Law — DevIQ)
  • Consumers have no baseline to push back from. They accept whatever the industry says they need. (Suzuki)

reflecting on the argument
Original Argument

What are the consequences of a computing industry that advances faster than the consumers it serves?

Finding

Consumers got genuine value and lost their ability to evaluate the industry simultaneously. The baseline shifted so far that the question of whether it was good or bad is almost impossible to answer from inside it.

solutions

What would change the equation

01
Hardware Longevity Standards
Mandatory minimum support periods. EU requires 10 years of spare parts. (EU Right to Repair Directive)
02
Tech Literacy in Schools
Teaching hardware history gives students the reference point that generational amnesia denies them. (Suzuki)
03
Right to Repair
FTC called manufacturer restrictions anticompetitive in 2021. Repair access disrupts the upgrade treadmill. (FTC — Nixing the Fix)
04
Software Transparency
Require software to disclose actual vs. recommended specs. Make Wirth's Law visible so consumers can push back.
05
Digital Equity Programs
Long-term funding for access programs. Ending the ACP cut off 23M households in June 2024. (FCC)
The computing industry advanced faster than consumers for fifty years.
Each acceleration permanently reset what users consider normal — and erased their ability to question it.
The most important consequence isn't the e-waste or the cost. It's that the industry now controls what you think you need.
